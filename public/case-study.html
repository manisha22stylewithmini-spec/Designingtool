Case study · Product design

One free studio for every social post — static, carousel and reel.

Delulu Craft is a design and animation studio for people who post for a living or for love. It has no ads, no paid tier and no locked features. This is the thinking behind it: who it is for, what hurt, and why each decision was made.

01 — POINT OF VIEW

Making a good post should not require a subscription, three tools, or a tutorial.

My belief: a creator's only real cost should be their time. When a tool charges for the basics — resizing, brand colours, a clean export — it quietly decides who gets to look professional. I wanted to build the version where that decision is not made for you.

For whom

Solo creators, students, small-brand owners and freelancers who run several brands or accounts and post in more than one format.

Job to be done

"When I have an idea for a post, help me turn it into the right size and format for the right platform, fast, and find it again later."

Promise

No ads. No paid plan. No feature locked behind an upgrade. Clear enough that you never wonder what a button does.

02 — THE PROBLEM

Posting is a pipeline, and the tools treat it as separate jobs.

A single idea often becomes an Instagram carousel, a story, a reel cover, a LinkedIn post and a thumbnail. Each platform wants a different size, and each format wants a different kind of tool: a layout tool for static posts, a vector tool for custom graphics, a video editor for reels, a spreadsheet or folder tree for keeping track.

The result is a workflow made of exports, re-imports and re-sizing. The creator is doing the tools' integration work by hand.

The problem is not "making a design". It is moving one idea across formats, platforms and brands without losing the thread.

How I framed it. I treated this as a continuity problem — keeping one idea intact from sketch to export to archive — rather than a feature-gap problem. That framing is what led to a unified editor and a library organised by brand, date and platform, instead of yet another template gallery.

03 — PAIN POINTS

Six pain points, each with a design response.

Sources: my own posting workflow, a walk-through of mainstream design tools, and feedback while the product was being built. Add 3–5 real quotes or session notes here if you have them.

1

Every platform wants a different size

Sizes change by platform and by format (feed, story, cover, banner). People guess, crop badly, or search "Instagram size 2025" every time.

BeforeRemember pixel sizes; resize by hand; things get cropped.
ResponseEvery platform and variant is a named preset with its use ("takes the most feed space"), safe areas for stories, and any design can be converted to another size.
2

Static, carousel and reel live in different tools

Moving a still post into a reel, or a carousel into separate posts, means starting again.

BeforeThree tools, three file formats, three sets of habits.
ResponseOne editor with post type as a switch: Static, Carousel, Video. The same layers, fonts and brand kit work in all three.
3

The basics sit behind a paywall or an ad

In the mainstream tools I reviewed, conveniences such as brand kits, one-click resizing and some exports sit on paid plans, and free tiers lean on upsell prompts. (Verify against each tool's current plans before you publish this page.)

BeforeFree until the moment you need the thing you came for.
ResponseNothing is gated. Fonts, resizing, brand kits, masks, a timeline and export are all included, and there is no ad inventory in the interface.
4

Finished work gets lost

Designs end up in one flat "recent" list. When you manage several brands, finding "the LinkedIn carousel from last Tuesday" is archaeology.

BeforeA flat grid sorted by last edit.
ResponseA library that mirrors how people think: Brand → Date → Platform → Type → Creatives, with breadcrumbs and a back button on every level.
5

Pro tools are powerful but hostile to a quick post

Vector and motion tools have the controls creators want (pen tool, anchor editing, masks, keyframes) but bury them in dense panels built for specialists.

BeforeChoose between easy and capable.
ResponsePower is available but not in your face: the right panel appears only when something is selected, the left panel collapses, and a status bar says what the current mode does.
6

Assets are a licensing and cost trap

Fonts, music and stock images are where "free" tools quietly become paid, or where creators get copyright claims after posting.

BeforePremium fonts, music packs and stock are upsells.
ResponseOpen-licence Google Fonts loaded on demand, a music library synthesised in the browser (so there is nothing to license), and import from your own device for anything else.
04 — THE CONSTRAINT

"No ads, no subscription" was a design constraint, not a slogan.

Deciding there would be no revenue model forced a set of decisions that a monetised product would never make. Each one is a trade-off I chose on purpose.

Because there is no…The product has to…What it cost
Ad incomeKeep the interface free of promos, banners and "sponsored" templates, so the canvas gets all the attention.No sponsored content to fund features.
SubscriptionInclude every feature for everyone; never use a lock icon as a nudge.No recurring revenue to pay for servers.
Server budgetBe local-first: designs, history, uploads and exports live in the browser, so running it costs almost nothing.No automatic cloud sync (see Trade-offs).
Licensing budgetUse open-licence fonts, synthesised music and user-supplied media.A smaller, less glamorous asset library than paid stock.
Data to sellCollect only what sign-in needs; keep creative work out of any analytics pipeline.Less usage data to learn from, so I rely on direct feedback.
05 — KEY DECISIONS

The decisions that shaped the product.

Each decision lists what I considered, why I chose what I did, and what it costs.

Core model

Post type is a feature, not a step

Considered
A 1-2-3-4 guided wizard (pick platform → format → size → start).
Chose
A visible switch for Static / Carousel / Video that can be changed any time.
Why
My first version used a step guide. Feedback was blunt: it repeated information and treated a choice as a process. People do not move through post types in order; they pick one and sometimes change their mind. A switch respects that.
Cost
The switch has to explain itself, so each option carries a short description of what it does.
Mental models

"Static" does not mean "one post"; "Carousel" means connected

Considered
Treating static as a single frame and carousel as a long strip.
Chose
Static = many independent designs, each with its own size. Carousel = slides that share one size and can share a stretched background. A Fixed / Free canvas toggle sets how much freedom you get.
Why
Creators batch several one-off posts in a sitting, and they want carousel slides to feel like one continuous piece. Two different mental models need two different behaviours.
Cost
More states to explain. I limited the "gap between frames" control to Free mode on static designs only, because it is irrelevant elsewhere and would add noise.
Simplify

Animated posts and reels are the same thing

Considered
Separate "Animated post" and "Reel" types.
Chose
One Video type with size choices (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9).
Why
The difference was only the frame size. Two names for one workflow is a tax on the user's memory.
Cost
None found; removing the duplicate made the picker clearer.
Information architecture

A library that mirrors how people remember work

Considered
A flat recents grid; folders the user manages by hand; tags.
Chose
Automatic filing by Brand → Date → Platform → Type, shown as cards with filters, breadcrumbs and a Back control on every level.
Why
People recall "which brand, roughly when, which platform". Auto-filing removes the chore of organising, while breadcrumbs make the depth safe to explore. Location is also in the URL, so the browser's own back button works.
Cost
Strict hierarchy is less flexible than free-form tags. Search (⌘K) covers the gap.
Brand └─ Date (cards, filterable) └─ Platform (Instagram · LinkedIn · Behance · …) └─ Type (Static · Carousel · Video) └─ Creatives
Focus

Hide what is not in use

Considered
Always-visible property panels like pro tools.
Chose
The right panel appears only when something is selected; the left panel collapses and explains how to bring it back; a status bar names the current mode.
Why
An empty properties panel taking a third of the screen was an early complaint. The canvas is the product, so everything else should earn its space.
Cost
Less discoverability of properties; mitigated with on-canvas hints and a toast after key actions.
Power, gently

Pro tools, introduced progressively

Considered
Keeping the product "simple" by leaving advanced tools out.
Chose
Pen and pencil with Figma-style anchor editing (double-click an anchor to change corners), masks from shapes or paths, crop handles, scale, canvas expand/trim, and an After-Effects-style keyframe timeline.
Why
The people I design for do reach for these, and sending them to another tool is exactly the fragmentation I set out to remove. Using familiar interactions from tools they already know lowers the learning cost.
Cost
More surface area to test and maintain; each tool had to degrade gracefully when unused.
Reuse

Templates are live layers, and they follow any size

Considered
Flat template images with a text overlay.
Chose
Every template is real layers — photo, text, panels. When the design is resized, the photo still covers the frame and text sitting on a screen or board stays on that spot of the photo.
Why
A template you cannot edit is a poster, not a starting point. Layout conversion has to preserve intent, not just scale numbers.
Cost
Text baked into a source photo cannot be edited; I cover it with an editable panel where I can.
Trust

Copy and paste work like the rest of your computer

Considered
An in-app clipboard only.
Chose
Real system clipboard: items paste across designs and tabs; text, screenshots, image links and videos paste in from anywhere; copied items also go out as text and a picture.
Why
A creative tool that breaks ⌘C / ⌘V loses trust instantly. I also left normal copy/paste untouched inside text fields.
Cost
Browsers differ (Safari and Firefox handle image clipboard writes differently), so I tested the common paths and noted the limits.
Memory

History is saved automatically, per design

Chose
Every design keeps its own saved history in the browser, with a History panel to go back.
Why
Creative work is exploratory; fear of losing a version slows people down. Safety should be invisible and automatic.
Cost
Stored locally, so clearing browser data erases it (see Trade-offs).
06 — THE CORE FLOW

From idea to export without changing tools.

Sign in→Home: big “+”→Pick platform & size, or a template→Choose Static / Carousel / Video→Edit on canvas→Auto-saved to Library→Export PNG · JPG · ZIP · video

The Home screen puts a large create button first, then quick-create tiles by format, then trending ideas, then the user's brands and recent designs. The order reflects frequency: most visits are "start something new" or "continue the last thing".

08 — WHAT I GOT WRONG

Corrections made along the way.

The step guide

I built a numbered flow that duplicated the post-type choice. It was removed and replaced by the switch described above.

Empty slides were purple

A branded gradient on a blank frame looked designed but confused users about what would be exported. Empty frames are now plain white.

Always-on right panel

Showing properties with nothing selected wasted space. It now appears on selection only.

Two names for video

"Animated" and "Reel" merged into a single Video type with size options.

Gap control everywhere

Offered on every mode at first. Now limited to static designs in Free canvas, where it solves a real carousel-building need.

Resize broke backgrounds

Early resizing scaled photos uniformly and left gaps. Full-frame photos and panels now keep covering the frame, and layers placed on a photo stay on it.

09 — HONEST TRADE-OFFS

What this approach costs the user.

Limit

Work lives in one browser

Local-first keeps it free and private, but there is no automatic cloud sync between devices, and clearing site data removes designs. Planned mitigation: export / import of a project file, then optional sync that the user opts into.

Limit

Smaller asset library

Open-licence fonts and synthesised music are safe but not as rich as paid stock. Users can import their own.

Limit

Browser differences

Some clipboard and video-recording features behave differently across browsers; Chrome is the most complete.

Gain

Nothing to upgrade to

The user never meets a locked feature, a watermark or an ad. That predictability is the product's main promise.

11 — REFLECTION

What I would carry forward.

  • Frame the problem as continuity, not features. It led to a unified editor and a library built around memory.
  • A constraint can be a design brief. "No ads, no subscription" shaped storage, assets and priorities more than any feature list did.
  • Listen for repeated explanations. Every time I had to explain a control, it needed to be removed or renamed.
  • Be loud about limits. Saying clearly that work lives in your browser builds more trust than hiding it.
