My point of view

Looking professional online should not depend on what you can pay. A creator's only cost should be their time.

The problem

One idea, five formats, three tools.

A single post idea becomes a carousel, a story, a reel cover and a LinkedIn post. Each needs a different size and, usually, a different tool. Creators do the integration by hand: export, re-import, resize, lose track.

1

Sizes are a memory test

Every platform and format wants different dimensions.

2

Formats are split across tools

Static, carousel and video rarely share a workspace.

3

Basics get paywalled

Brand kits, resizing and exports sit behind plans or upsell prompts.

4

Work gets lost

A flat “recent” list fails once you manage several brands.

5

Pro tools are hostile

Powerful, but built for specialists, not a quick post.

6

Assets hide costs

Fonts, music and stock become upsells or copyright risks.

The solution

Pain point → design response.

Pain

I never remember platform sizes.

Solution

Every platform and variant is a named preset with a plain-language use, safe areas for stories, and one-click conversion to any other size.

Pain

Static, carousel and reels need different tools.

Solution

One editor with a Static / Carousel / Video switch. Same layers, fonts and brand kit everywhere.

Pain

The basics are locked or interrupted by ads.

Solution

Everything is included for everyone; the interface has no ad space and no lock icons.

Pain

I cannot find last week's LinkedIn carousel.

Solution

A library filed automatically as Brand → Date → Platform → Type, with breadcrumbs, Back and ⌘K search.

Pain

Pro tools are too much for a quick post.

Solution

Pen, masks, crop and a keyframe timeline are there, but panels appear only when you select something.

Pain

Fonts and music cost money or risk claims.

Solution

Open-licence Google Fonts, music synthesised in the browser, and import from your own device.

Constraint as a brief

No ads. No subscription. Here is what that forced.

Adsnone

With no revenue model, the product had to be cheap to run, so it is local-first: designs, history and uploads live in the browser. It had to be free of licensing cost, so it uses open fonts and synthesised music. The trade-off is honest: no automatic cloud sync yet — the full case study explains how I planned for it.

Key decisions

Four calls that shaped it.

Post type is a feature, not a step

My first guided flow repeated itself. Replacing it with one clear switch matched how people actually work: pick a type, change your mind.

Library mirrors memory

People remember brand, roughly when, and platform — so files sort themselves that way, with breadcrumbs to make depth safe.

Hide what is not in use

The properties panel appears only on selection; the left panel collapses. The canvas gets the space.

Templates are live layers

Every layer is editable and follows the design to any size, so a template is a starting point, not a poster.

Outcome

What shipped.

  • Static, carousel and video in one editor, with presets for every major platform.
  • Pen, masks, crop, timeline, text effects, 60 fonts and a built-in music library.
  • A home dashboard, template gallery and a library filed by brand, date and platform.
  • Automatic saved history; copy and paste that works with the rest of your computer.
  • 20 photo templates with fully editable layers.
